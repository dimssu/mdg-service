# 2 · The customer

### What a petrol pump dealer is

A roadside filling station in India is **not owned by the fuel brand on the
canopy.** It is run by a private **dealer** under a dealership agreement with an
oil marketing company. The oil company sets the rules, runs the portals,
supplies the fuel on credit, and inspects.

The dealer carries an obligation nobody outside the forecourt sees: a constant
stream of filings, renewals, inspections and deadlines, most of it landing
personally on the owner.

### The person

A man of forty-plus who owns or runs the pump. He has run it for twenty years
and knows the trade completely. He has very little to do with technology and
mild suspicion toward it. **He is not stupid — he is busy, and he has been sold
to before.**

The company's own persona notes: low digital confidence, cheap Android phone,
patchy network, **abandons at the first moment of confusion or embarrassment**,
and wants above all **not to feel stupid**. Frustrated by menus, logins, jargon
and English-heavy screens. A secondary persona is a manager in her thirties with
medium tech comfort.

**He is not buying software. He is buying a job that leaves his hands and does
not come back.** The most valuable idea in the company's marketing is that the
work can be done by his manager, without him.

**His fear is not the price. It is being handed something he cannot ask a
question about.**

### Why he says no

Not formally researched. Inferred from the contract, the product requirements doc
(`docs/PRD.md`) and the company's own notes:

1. He must hand over control of things he holds personally. This is the largest
   trust ask in the product and no marketing surface addresses it.
2. He cannot easily verify the work is being done. The agreement states the
   provider has no mechanism to check the authenticity of what the dealer
   supplies, and disclaims liability for penalties.
3. It is a one-year auto-renewing commitment.
4. "I already have a man who does this."
5. Embarrassment — not wanting to be the man who could not work the app.

# 4 · The stock variation rule

**The one regulatory rule the company can state as fact.** Founder-confirmed as
a genuine industry rule, and the only clause quoted with its own text anywhere
in the company's material: **clause 5.1.11, "Stock variation of MS/HSD beyond
permissible limits."**

**The band is 4% of tank stock**, plus — **on a shortage only** — an evaporation
and handling allowance on the quantity sold: 0.75% for petrol, 0.25% for diesel
(slightly lower for outlets above a 600 KL annual average).

**The allowance is two things, not one, and which way the gap runs matters.**

> 10,000 L in the tank and 20,000 L of diesel sold.
> Band = 400 L (4% of stock) + 50 L (evaporation on sales) = **450 L**.
> On an **excess** the evaporation half does not apply, so the band is **400 L**.
> Fuel does not evaporate *into* a tank.

**Consequences, as the company states them:**

- **Shortage beyond the band** — samples go for lab testing, sales continue, and
  the dealer's **written explanation is called for**. Supply is suspended only
  if the explanation is unsatisfactory.
- **Excess beyond the band is worse** — samples go for lab testing and **sales
  and supply of all products are suspended immediately.**

**Nothing else about oil company consequences may be stated.** What happens on a
missed credit deadline, at what threshold, or what fine applies is not visible
in anything the company can see. Do not guess at a penalty.

## What the dealer actually receives

### The daily sales report — the centre of the offer

Two phone-sized images. He copies the rows into his own paper register — and
**anyone at the pump can do it. It does not have to be him.**

**Panel one, the sheet.** The day's row and the day before, in his own
register's column order: DATE · per tank DIP, WATER DIP, STOCK · OPENING STOCK ·
RECEIPT · TOTAL STOCK · OPENING METRE READING per nozzle · TESTING · SALES ·
CUMULATIVE SALES for the month · REMARKS. A glance strip on top gives yesterday's
sales, stock in tank now, and what was received.

Two details that matter: **today's SALES cell is always empty** — it closes on
tomorrow's reading. And a delivery entered by hand, or worked out from a rise in
the tank dip rather than a receipt, is marked as such on the row.

**Panel two, the stock check.** A plain verdict — *stock is fine*, *stock is
short*, *stock is over* — with the permissible band shown as its two parts
rather than one unexplained number, the gap, and one line saying what to do
about it. When the variation is beyond the band, the clause and its consequence
are printed underneath.

Illustrative figures used in the company's own material, invented and internally
consistent, from no real outlet:

| DATE | OPENING | RECEIPT | TOTAL | DIP | STOCK | TESTING | SALES |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 19-08 | 9,120 | 9,000 | 18,120 | 141 | 12,480 | 10 | 5,630 |
| 20-08 | 12,480 | 6,000 | 18,480 | 160 | 14,246 | 12 | 4,222 |

12,480 + 6,000 = 18,480; 18,480 − 14,246 = 4,234 gross, less 12 L testing =
**4,222 sold**. Row one closes into row two.

**Generation is automatic. Delivery is not** — a person at MDG checks the report
before it is sent. A corrected report carries a line saying it replaces the one
sent earlier for the same day.

### In detail

**Water testing record.** A statutory check for water in the tanks has to be
logged **in its own two-hour window** — twelve windows a day at an outlet that
runs round the clock, every day, forever. Filled at nine in the morning for the
midnight window, it is not a late entry: **it is a false one.** And once an entry
is saved there is no taking it back.

There is deliberately no catching up. **A window that closes unmarked stays
unmarked** — the record says what actually happened, not what would look tidy.

The dealer receives a day card: how many windows were recorded out of how many,
how many were missed, the outlet's operating hours, and when the last check was.
Under it, a row per window: the time slot, whether it was recorded, whether water
was found, and when it was entered. Missed windows carry the line *"a window can
only be recorded inside its own time, so these can no longer be entered."*

This is the most relentless daily obligation in the whole product and the least
talked about.

> **How this record comes to be made must never be described in public.** State
> the outcome only — that every testing window stays accounted for.

**Ledger Watch — what came off the account that was not fuel.** Live since
September 2026.

The dealer's running account with the oil company should be one routine pair
repeating: fuel bought, money deposited. It is not. Other things move money on
that statement quietly — interest on credit that ran long, licence fee
recoveries, commission returned, card settlements — and a dealer reading a
month-end balance has no easy way to see which line was which.

Ledger Watch classifies every line and flags everything outside the routine pair.
The dealer gets a card showing his **last four non-routine entries** — the date
each carried on the statement, what it was in plain English and Hindi, and the
amount. **Totals on the card are of those four only, and say so**, because a card
gets forwarded and argued with weeks later.

The card is explicitly labelled **"यह DOD जमा राशि नहीं है / This is not your DOD
deposit amount"** — the one thing it must never be mistaken for.

What the first run across the whole customer base actually found: **3,163 lines
classified into twelve kinds, of which only two are fuel bought and money
deposited.** Over a thousand lines were worth a second look; exactly one was
serious. Two patterns recur — the same named charge landing at wildly different
amounts at different pumps, and a commission credit arriving at a small fraction
of what comparable outlets received the same month.

**Tanker density.** When a load lands, its invoice prints a density figure per
product. The dealer must copy it into his density register that day. He is sent
the figure and the invoice is kept on file.

One deliberate limit: **the acknowledgement of a delivery is never done for
him.** That is a legal act by the dealer about a load he has inspected, and it
stays his.

**Compliance standing.** A percentage, what is still pending, what MDG has not
yet checked, and what MDG needs a photo of. Presented as **MDG's written
statement about that dealer** — never as a certification, and never as a
guarantee of passing an inspection.

## Per-tanker profit — an admin tool, not an app feature

**This does not live in the dealer's app.** It is a screen MDG's own team uses,
which produces a single image an admin can hand to a dealer.

**The question it answers** is the one a dealer actually asks and a monthly total
cannot answer: *"what did I make on that load?"* It prices **each tanker delivery
separately**.

**Why it is not automatic, and never will be.** Nothing in the system publishes a
fuel price — not the forecourt automation, not the oil company's portal, not the
inspection report, not the dealer's own workbook. So a profit figure computed
without a person would rest on a rate nobody could see. The rates are typed in by
hand, held only on the machine they were typed on, and the server is built to
refuse to return a rupee figure at all.

**The shape of the answer, per grade:**

> gross margin = litres × (pump price − landed cost)
> fuel lost = litres × the loss rate
> profit = gross margin + the value of the fuel lost

**The two honest uncertainties, both shown beside the answer rather than hidden:**

1. **What happened to the testing fuel.** The daily report assumes it was poured
   back into the tank, so it is neither revenue nor loss. If it was actually
   sold, it was never missing — and at a busy outlet that is most of the
   reported shortage. Nobody measures testing, so this is a genuine open
   question. **The card always prints both answers side by side**, because
   printing only one would forward a guess as a fact. It is also not a free win:
   at one outlet, treating testing as sold turned a petrol shortage into a
   surplus, and a surplus beyond the band is an immediate sales-suspension
   event.
2. **What a lost litre is worth.** At what it cost, which is the standard
   accounting treatment and the smaller number; or at the pump price, counting
   the margin he would have earned as forgone too — which is how a dealer
   instinctively reckons it. **There is one profit and two correct ways to
   explain it.** The toggle changes the explanation, never the figure.

> Worked example: 10,000 L bought at ₹90, pump price ₹95, 100 L vanish.
> The profit is **₹40,500** either way.
> At cost: margin on the 9,900 that sold, less 100 × ₹90.
> At retail: margin on all 10,000 bought, less 100 × ₹95.
> Mix the two and you get ₹41,000, which is wrong — and the table stops adding up.

**One guard worth knowing.** Above about half a percent of every litre received,
a *surplus* stops being plausible. A tank showing more fuel than the books
account for at that rate is almost never a windfall — it is a delivery that was
never reported, and counting it as profit draws exactly the wrong lesson. *(This
is MDG's own threshold, not an industry rule.)*

**A real landed cost, for scale:** on one tanker invoice, one day, one state,
diesel landed at about **₹97.76 a litre**. The petrol figure from the same
invoice implies a margin far thinner than the ₹2.50–3.00 normally quoted, and
**that has never been confirmed** — do not publish a petrol margin.

## The compliance load

**45 items on the clock at one outlet, across 9 different repeat cycles** — from
a daily check to one that comes round once in two years. Grouped into seven
areas: daily operations, documentation and display, portal filings, safety,
cleanliness, statutory licences, equipment.

**Ten are daily, and they are his morning:**

> Update today's sales report · update the density book · update the stock board
> — **all three before 10 am** · fill the toilet inspection sheet · fill the
> bathroom display sheet outside · keep stock variation within limit · display
> the product neatly on the island · complete the safety declaration · keep
> insurance renewed · keep the quality and quantity kit ready.

The rest run weekly, fortnightly, monthly, quarterly, half-yearly, yearly and
once in two years. Among them: the quarterly mock drill, the trade licence, the
Weights & Measures certificate, the quarterly tax filing, cleaning the
underground tank chamber, servicing the air compressor, cleaning the solar
panels, numbering the earthing pits, the insulation mat in the panel room,
hydrometer calibration, road NOC, staff ID cards and photos, first-aid box,
complaint book, earthing clips and bucket.

**The heaviest single item on the sheet is also the rarest — it comes round once
in two years.** The job done least often is the one that goes missing.

*These schedules come from one working dealer's own assessment sheet, not an oil
company circular. The obligations are real; the schedules are that dealer's
house rule and must not be broadcast as an industry standard.*

**Who certifies what:** an MDG admin or an automation closes every item. The
dealer's photo is evidence, never the decision. There is deliberately no "mark
done" button in the app. Of the 45, only two can honestly be automated today;
about half are permanently a person with a camera.

## Onboarding, support, pricing

**Onboarding — seven days.** Day 1, one call, plain talk, no forms. Day 2–3, he
picks which modules to switch on and the price is locked in writing. Day 4–7,
the outlet is live. The first full inspection and filing cycle usually clears
inside two weeks.

**What onboarding needs from the dealer:** remarkably little — his own existing
workbook and one shift export. An outlet with no automation at all can still be
set up; its figures are entered by hand.

**Support — 9am to 9pm, every day including Sunday, in Hindi or English.** "A
person, not a menu. The same team each time." Outside those hours a message
waits for the desk and is picked up first thing. A callback is promised within
the hour. *Note: nothing in the system enforces business hours — it is a
staffing commitment, not an automated one.*

**Pricing.** One monthly subscription, priced in writing before anything begins.
The standard MDG service is **₹3,000 a month plus 18% GST**, one charge across
India. The full Kavach film says it in those words; the short says ₹3,000 a month
with GST on top. The website assistant quotes it too, and its approved answer adds a free first month and about ₹15,000
plus GST for a full package. Premium services are priced on the work, pump by
pump. Four promises: **Modular · Locked · Checked · Lifted** — "no surprise fees,
no mid-year escalation", and the same price for everyone rather than a quote per

# 9 · Standing rules on what may be published

Company policy, not preference.

**Outcomes only, never method.** Public material says what the dealer
**receives**, never how it is produced — no logins, credentials, portals, data
collection, schedules or internal tooling. Neither confirm nor deny it; simply
never raise it. The stated reason: method on a marketing surface helps a
competitor and helps no customer. This rule was issued after internal screens
appeared on a public site.

**Two things in particular must never be described**, only their outcome: how
the water-testing record comes to be made, and anything the company does on a
dealer's behalf inside an oil company system.

**No internal or admin screens** in any public material. The only product
imagery permitted is screens a dealer already sees.

**Phone.** The toll-free 1800-891-3496 is published again. The parent company's
site (vruoom.com) prints it too. mdgservices.in removed it on 23 Aug 2026 and restored it on
9 Oct as one choice in the call sheet, beside the in-page call. The full Kavach
film's end card shows it, written and never spoken. The pamphlet and the marketing
film carry no phone number.

**No other company's mark.** No oil company logo, canopy livery, tanker
branding, uniform, signage or vehicle number plate. Indian Oil may be **named in
words** where a sentence needs it; it may not be shown as a mark. No national
flag or Ashoka Chakra in commercial creative.

**No real dealer's data.** No outlet code, no SAP code, no invoice number, no
real figures. Every number published is invented.

**No unverifiable consequence.** Never state what the oil company does when a
deadline is missed. One exception is safe because it is observable: miss the
quarterly mock drill and the next fuel order can be held.

**No testimonials, no dealer names, no case studies** — none exist yet.
